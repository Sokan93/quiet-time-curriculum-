Week 1: The Call to Be Different

Key Scriptures: Romans 12:1–2; Matthew 5:10–16; Romans 8:29; Numbers 14

Day 1 — Monday

Called to Be Different

Romans 12:1 (KJV) "I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service."

1. What Does It Mean to Be Different?

Being different as a Christian means choosing God's ways even when they differ from the ways of the world. It means allowing our beliefs, values, speech, decisions, and lifestyle to reflect Christ. A believer should not simply follow what everyone else is doing.

Key lesson: You do not need to behave like everyone else to be accepted. Your identity in Christ makes you different.

2. The Danger of Conformity

Romans 12:2 warns believers not to be conformed to this world. Conformity occurs when a person allows society, friends, trends, or peer pressure to determine how they live — doing wrong because friends are doing it, cheating because everyone else cheats, or compromising Christian values to gain acceptance.

Key lesson: The fact that something is popular does not mean that it is right.

3. Renewing the Mind

Transformation begins in the mind. What we constantly watch, hear, read, and think about can influence our behaviour. A Christian should fill the mind with God's Word, prayer, positive and godly thoughts, and good Christian fellowship. When the mind changes, attitudes and actions begin to change.

4. Presenting Ourselves as Living Sacrifices

This means surrendering our lives completely to God — our time, talents, bodies, possessions, relationships, plans, and future.

5. Discovering God's Will

A transformed mind helps us understand what God wants for our lives.

Day 1 conclusion: God has called us to stand apart from the world — not to copy it, but to demonstrate His will through our lives.

Day 2 — Tuesday

Different in Character and Conduct

Matthew 5:10 (KJV) "Blessed are they which are persecuted for righteousness' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven."

1. Being the Salt of the Earth

As Christians, our presence should make a positive difference wherever we are — at school, work, home, church, or among friends. Question: If every Christian behaved exactly like you, would society become better or worse?

2. Being the Light of the World

Our light can be seen through honesty, kindness, forgiveness, love, purity, respect, integrity, and compassion.

3. Living as an Example

People may not read the Bible, but they may "read" our lives. Our behaviour can either attract people to Christ or drive them away.

4. Standing for Righteousness

Being different sometimes means standing alone. Being different may attract criticism, but pleasing God should matter more than pleasing people.

5. Letting Our Good Works Glorify God

Our purpose is not to show off or seek praise — our good behaviour should point people toward God.

Day 2 conclusion: A Christian should be a positive influence — bringing flavour as salt and direction as light.

Day 3 — Wednesday

Different Because We Are Called to Be Like Christ

Romans 8:29 (KJV) "For whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be the firstborn among many brethren."

1. God's Purpose for Every Believer

God does not only want us to receive blessings; He wants us to become more like Christ. The goal of Christianity is not simply wealth, success, popularity, or position — it is also character transformation.

2. What Is Christlikeness?

Love, humility, compassion, patience, forgiveness, obedience, self-control, faithfulness, and sacrifice.

3. Developing the Character of Christ

We grow through studying God's Word, prayer, obedience, fellowship, trials, and learning from our mistakes.

4. Love, Humility, and Obedience

Being different means choosing love when others choose hatred, humility when others seek pride, and obedience when others choose rebellion.

5. Representing Christ in Our Generation

Our generation needs believers who demonstrate Christ through their lives rather than merely speak about Him.

Day 3 conclusion: The greatest evidence that we are different is the character of Christ displayed in our lives.

Day 4 — Thursday

Different in Faith and Courage

Numbers 14:1 (KJV) "And all the congregation lifted up their voice, and cried; and the people wept that night."

1. The Story of the Twelve Spies

Ten spies returned with a negative report, focused on the giants and difficulties. Joshua and Caleb believed God was able to give them the land — they saw the same land but responded differently.

2. Joshua and Caleb Had a Different Spirit

Being different does not necessarily mean seeing something different — sometimes it means responding differently to the same situation.

3. Refusing to Follow the Crowd

Being in the majority does not always mean being right. Sometimes you must stand alone for truth.

4. Faith Instead of Fear

Fear says "I cannot." Faith says "God is able."

5. The Reward of Standing With God

Joshua and Caleb were rewarded for their faithfulness — they entered the Promised Land.

Day 4 conclusion: Never allow the fear or unbelief of others to destroy your faith.

Day 5 — Friday

Dare to Be Different: Living Out Our Calling

Matthew 5:13 (KJV) "Ye are the salt of the earth: but if the salt have lost his savour, wherewith shall it be salted? it is thenceforth good for nothing, but to be cast out, and to be trodden under foot of men."

1. Counting the Cost of Being Different

You may experience rejection, mockery, criticism, peer pressure, loneliness, and opposition. Following Christ requires commitment.

2. Overcoming Peer Pressure

A Christian must learn to say: "No, this is not who I am, and this is not what God wants for me."

3. Standing for Truth

Truth must remain truth even when unpopular — honesty, purity, justice, love, righteousness, respect, God's Word.

4. Influencing Others Positively

Your words can encourage someone, your character can inspire someone, your faith can strengthen someone.

5. Becoming a Different Generation

A generation that honours God, rejects ungodliness, stands against corruption, and walks in holiness.

6. Final Challenge

Will I follow the crowd, or will I follow Christ? Will I hide my light, or will I shine?

Final Theme Statement "We are called not to fit into the world, but to stand out for Christ; not to follow the crowd, but to follow God's Word; not to hide our light, but to shine for His glory."

Week 2: Contentment

Day 1 — Monday

Understanding True Contentment

1 Timothy 6:6 (KJV) "But godliness with contentment is great gain."

1. What Is Contentment?

Contentment is a state of being satisfied and peaceful with what God has given us while trusting Him for what we do not yet have. A contented person can say, "I may not have everything I desire, but I am grateful for what God has given me."

2. Contentment vs. Complacency

Complacency means being satisfied to the extent that one no longer makes effort to grow. A contented Christian can still work hard, build a career, or seek spiritual growth without allowing the desire for more to destroy their peace.

3. Why Godliness With Contentment Is Great Gain

Material possessions can disappear, but a godly and contented heart gives lasting peace.

4. Learning to Be Satisfied With What God Has Provided

We should thank God for life, family, health, opportunities, food, shelter, relationships, and talents.

5. The Dangers of a Restless and Dissatisfied Heart

Constant dissatisfaction can lead to envy, jealousy, greed, and bitterness.

Lesson: A truly rich person is not necessarily the one who has the most, but the one who is grateful for what God has given.

Day 2 — Tuesday

Contentment Through the Fear of the Lord

Proverbs 15:16 (KJV) "Better is little with the fear of the LORD than great treasure and trouble therewith."

1. The Value of Having the Fear of the Lord

A person who fears God understands that material success without God's approval is ultimately empty.

2. Why Little With God Is Better Than Much Without Him

Money can provide comfort, but it cannot guarantee peace, good relationships, good health, or eternal life.

3. Contentment vs. Material Abundance

The problem arises when possessions become more important than God.

4. Developing Gratitude for God's Daily Provisions

Gratitude changes our focus from "What don't I have?" to "What has God already given me?"

5. How the Fear of God Produces Peace and Satisfaction

We do not have to destroy ourselves competing with everyone around us.

Lesson: Peace with God is more valuable than wealth without God.

Day 3 — Wednesday

The Danger of Covetousness and Endless Desire

Proverbs 27:20 (KJV) "Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied."

1. Understanding Covetousness

Covetousness makes a person believe, "I cannot be happy until I have what someone else has."

2. Why the Human Heart Is Never Satisfied

Without contentment, every achievement can simply create another desire.

3. The Dangers of Comparison and Unhealthy Competition

Social media can worsen this because people often display successes without showing struggles.

4. How Uncontrolled Desires Destroy Contentment

Desire becomes dangerous when it controls our thoughts and decisions.

5. Learning to Appreciate What We Have While Trusting God for What We Need

We can tell God about our needs without allowing them to become idols in our hearts.

Lesson: If we do not learn to be satisfied, getting more will never be enough.

Day 4 — Thursday

Contentment in God Alone

Psalm 73:25 (KJV) "Whom have I in heaven but thee? and there is none upon earth that I desire beside thee."

1. Making God Our Greatest Treasure

When God becomes our greatest treasure, material things assume their proper place.

2. Why Earthly Possessions Cannot Truly Satisfy

Wealth cannot replace God's presence, peace, love, or salvation.

3. Finding Satisfaction in God's Presence

His presence gives strength during difficulties, hope during uncertainty, and comfort during disappointment.

4. Trusting God When Circumstances Are Difficult

Contentment is tested most strongly during difficult seasons.

5. Developing a Heart That Says "God Is Enough"

We will not allow possessions, relationships, status, or achievements to determine our worth.

Lesson: When God becomes our greatest treasure, we become less controlled by what we don't have.

Day 5 — Friday

The Secret of Living a Contented Life

Psalm 73:26 (KJV) "My flesh and my heart faileth: but God is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever."

1–6. Depending on God, Accepting Every Season, Practising Gratitude

A contented Christian does not allow circumstances to determine their faith. We should enjoy God's blessings responsibly in abundance and trust Him in need, practising gratitude daily and avoiding comparison with others.

7. Conclusion: God Is the Strength of Our Heart and Our Portion Forever

Everything on earth can change or disappear, but God remains.

Final Thought "Contentment is not having everything you want; it is being grateful for everything God has given you while trusting Him for everything you still need."

Week 3: Discernment

Day 1 — Monday

Understanding Discernment and Recognising Deception

2 Corinthians 11:13 (KJV) "For such are false apostles, deceitful workers, transforming themselves into the apostles of Christ."

1. What Is Spiritual Discernment?

The ability to distinguish between what is true and false, what is from God and what is not, looking beyond outward appearances.

2. Why Christians Need Discernment

Not everything presented in the name of God is necessarily from God.

3. Recognising False Apostles and Deceptive Workers

Deception can come from people who use Christian language and appear religious.

4. How Deception Can Appear Genuine

A discerning Christian asks: Does this teaching agree with Scripture? Does it point people toward Christ?

5–7. Distinguishing Truth From Error; Not Believing Everything We Hear; Developing a Discerning Spirit

Truth must be measured against God's Word rather than emotions or personal preferences. Discernment grows through prayer, Scripture, maturity, and obedience.

Key lesson: Not everything that looks spiritual is from God.

Day 2 — Tuesday

Discernment Through God's Good and Perfect Gifts

James 1:17 (KJV) "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning."

Not everything attractive is God's will. Discernment helps us ask whether something agrees with God's character before accepting it, avoiding deception through offers that promise money, popularity, or quick success without cost.

Key lesson: Discernment teaches us to recognise God's blessings without being deceived by what merely appears attractive.

Day 3 — Wednesday

Discernment Through the Truth of God's Word

John 17:17 (KJV) "Sanctify them through thy truth: thy word is truth."

God's Word is a reliable standard for evaluating teachings, beliefs, and decisions. The important question is not "Who said it?" but "Is it consistent with God's Word?" False doctrine can influence beliefs, choices, and one's relationship with God.

Key lesson: The better we know God's Word, the better equipped we become to discern truth from error.

Day 4 — Thursday

The Power of God's Word in Discerning the Heart

Hebrews 4:12 (KJV) "For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword... and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart."

Scripture exposes hidden motives and helps us examine our inner life. True discernment requires humility — receiving correction rather than defensiveness when Scripture exposes something wrong in us.

Key lesson: God's Word reveals what is happening within us, not only around us.

Day 5 — Friday

Growing in Discernment and Spiritual Maturity

Ephesians 4:14 (KJV) "That we henceforth be no more children, tossed to and fro, and carried about with every wind of doctrine, by the sleight of men, and cunning craftiness, whereby they lie in wait to deceive."

Spiritually immature believers are easily deceived. Christians should test spiritual claims by their biblical foundation, fruit, and consistency with Christ's character — remaining stable, speaking truth in love rather than becoming harsh or judgmental.

Key lesson: Spiritual maturity makes us stable, wise, and less vulnerable to deception.

Week 4: Gratitude

Day 1 — Monday

The Will of God Concerning Gratitude

1 Thessalonians 5:18 (KJV) "In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you."

Gratitude is more than saying "thank you" — it is an attitude of the heart recognising that every good thing comes from God. Giving thanks "in every thing" does not mean everything that happens is good; it means we can trust God even in difficulty. Constant complaining causes us to forget what God has already done.

Key lesson: A grateful heart focuses on God's faithfulness rather than life's difficulties.

Day 2 — Tuesday

Recognising God as the Source of Every Good Gift

James 1:17 (KJV) "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning."

Although our efforts matter, gratitude recognises God's hand behind the opportunities and abilities that make achievements possible. Entitlement says "I deserve this"; gratitude says "I appreciate what I have received." Gratitude protects the heart from pride.

Key lesson: Never become so focused on God's gifts that you forget the Giver.

Day 3 — Wednesday

Entering God's Presence With Thanksgiving

Psalm 100:4 (KJV) "Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name."

We do not have to wait until every prayer is answered before thanking God. Remembering past faithfulness strengthens present faith, and thanksgiving keeps our hearts joyful rather than fixated on what is missing.

Key lesson: Remembering God's faithfulness gives us reasons to praise Him in every season.

Day 4 — Thursday

Giving Thanks in All Things Because God Works for Our Good

Romans 8:28 (KJV) "And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose."

This does not say everything that happens is good — it assures that God can work through all things for our good. Like Joseph, difficult experiences can become testimonies of God's faithfulness.

Key lesson: When we cannot understand God's plan, we can still trust His heart.

Day 5 — Friday

Replacing Anxiety With Prayer and Thanksgiving

Philippians 4:6 (KJV) "Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God."

Anxiety focuses on what could go wrong; gratitude redirects our attention to God's faithfulness. The pattern: Worry → Prayer → Thanksgiving → Trust → Peace.

Key lesson: When we replace worry with prayer and thanksgiving, we position our hearts to experience God's peace.

Final Charge "A grateful heart does not deny the existence of problems; it simply refuses to let problems become greater than God's goodness."

Week 5: Examination Culture

Day 1 — Monday

Understanding Examination Culture

An examination is a formal assessment used to determine a student's knowledge, understanding, skills, and abilities. Examination culture refers to the attitudes, behaviours, habits, and values students, teachers, and parents develop toward examinations. Types include class tests, continuous assessment, terminal, mock, and external examinations (WAEC, NECO).

Characteristics of a Positive Examination Culture

  • Regular reading and revision, punctuality, good time management
  • Honesty, obedience to instructions, confidence, hard work
  • Respect for teachers and officials, proper preparation
  • Avoidance of examination malpractice

Conclusion: Students should see examinations as opportunities to demonstrate learning rather than something to fear.

Day 2 — Tuesday

Effective Preparation for Examinations

Examination preparation is the process of getting ready mentally, physically, and academically. A good reading timetable allocates sufficient time to each subject, gives more attention to difficult subjects, includes breaks, revision, and adequate sleep. Regular revision and honest practice with past questions build familiarity and confidence.

Conclusion: Students who prepare early are more likely to approach examinations calmly and confidently.

Day 3 — Wednesday

Examination Ethics and Integrity

Examination malpractice is any dishonest or prohibited behaviour intended to gain an unfair advantage — including bringing unauthorised materials, copying, impersonation, or using unauthorised electronic devices. Causes include inadequate preparation, fear of failure, and peer pressure. Consequences can include cancellation of results, suspension, or expulsion.

Conclusion: It is better to obtain an honest result through hard work than an excellent result through cheating.

Day 4 — Thursday

Managing Examination Anxiety and Pressure

Examination anxiety is excessive worry, fear, or tension before or during an examination. Causes include poor preparation, comparison with classmates, and unrealistic expectations. Students can manage stress by preparing early, resting adequately, eating well, and talking to trusted teachers, parents, or counsellors.

Conclusion: Examinations should be approached with preparation rather than fear.

Day 5 — Friday

Building a Positive Examination Culture

A responsible examination candidate is hardworking, honest, disciplined, punctual, and respectful of rules. Responsibilities before an exam include studying consistently and resting; during, reading instructions carefully and managing time; after, learning from mistakes rather than dwelling on them.

General Conclusion A positive examination culture is built on preparation, discipline, honesty, confidence, hard work, perseverance, and respect for examination rules.

Week 7: Pushing the Limit

Day 1 — Monday

Knowing Your Purpose and Potential

Isaiah 54:2 (KJV) "Enlarge the place of your tent, stretch your tent curtains wide, do not hold back; lengthen your cords, strengthen your stakes."

1. Understanding "Pushing the Limit"

Making a deliberate effort to go beyond your present level of achievement — refusing to remain satisfied with poor habits, laziness, fear, or lack of discipline when you have the ability to improve.

2. Every Student Has Unique Abilities and Potential

Some students are gifted academically, others in music, sports, public speaking, writing, technology, or leadership. A talent that is not developed may remain hidden.

3. Discovering Your Purpose and God-Given Talents

Students should pray, study, seek wise counsel, and pay attention to what they enjoy and do well — not choose a path simply because friends are choosing it.

4. Developing a Positive Mindset

Replace "I cannot do it" with "I can improve," "I will ask for help," "I will keep practising."

5. Refusing to Let Fear or Failure Limit You

Failure can become a learning experience if a student examines what went wrong and tries again.

6. Setting Meaningful Goals

A good goal should be accompanied by a plan and consistent action.

Evaluation

  1. What does "pushing the limit" mean?
  2. Why should students discover their talents?
  3. Mention three things that can limit a student's potential.
  4. How can failure become useful?
  5. What does 1 Chronicles 28:9 teach us?

Conclusion: The first step in pushing the limit is recognising that you have potential and deciding to develop it.

Day 2 — Tuesday

Developing a Focused Mind

Psalm 27:4 (KJV) "One thing have I desired of the LORD, that will I seek after; that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the LORD, and to enquire in his temple."

1. The Importance of Focus and Concentration

A student may have excellent abilities but underperform because of poor concentration.

2. Making God the Centre of Your Ambitions

Before major decisions, ask: Does this honour God? Have I prayed about it? Have I sought wise counsel?

3. Avoiding Distractions

Excessive social media, gaming, procrastination, and negative friendships can hinder growth. A phone should be a tool, not something that controls the schedule.

4–5. Good Study Habits and Time Management

Balance academic work, prayer, household responsibilities, rest, exercise, and family time.

6. Staying Focused When Results Are Not Immediate

Consistency is often more powerful than occasional effort.

Evaluation

  1. What is focus?
  2. Mention five distractions that can affect students.
  3. Why is time management important?
  4. What distraction will you work on reducing?

Conclusion: A focused student is better prepared to turn dreams into reality.

Day 3 — Wednesday

Self-Examination and Personal Growth

Psalm 139:23 (KJV) "Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts."

1–2. Examining Yourself and Identifying Strengths and Weaknesses

Knowing your weaknesses is not a sign of failure — it gives you an opportunity to grow.

3. Recognising Limiting Habits

Laziness, procrastination, carelessness, anger, and refusal to accept correction can prevent students from reaching their potential.

4. Accepting Correction

A teachable student listens, considers advice, and makes appropriate changes.

5–6. Learning From Mistakes and Developing Humility

A humble student can say "I don't understand," "I made a mistake," "I need help."

Evaluation

  1. What is self-examination?
  2. Mention three of your strengths and three areas needing improvement.
  3. How can failure become a learning opportunity?

Conclusion: When you are willing to examine yourself, you create room for personal growth.

Day 4 — Thursday

Checking Your Motives

Proverbs 16:2 (KJV) "All the ways of a man are clean in his own eyes; but the LORD weigheth the spirits."

1–2. Understanding Motives; Good vs. Selfish Motives

Good motives: desire to learn, improve, help others, honour God. Wrong motives: desire for attention, praise, or success through dishonesty.

3. Pursuing Success Without Pride or Envy

Healthy competition encourages hard work; it becomes unhealthy when students sabotage others to win.

4. Doing Right Even When Nobody Is Watching

True character is revealed by what we do when we believe nobody is watching.

5–6. Balancing Ambition With Integrity

A student who gets the highest grade through cheating has not achieved true success.

Evaluation

  1. What is a motive?
  2. Mention three good motives and three wrong motives.
  3. Why is character important in addition to academic success?

Conclusion: True success is not simply achieving great things; it is achieving them while maintaining good character.

Day 5 — Friday

Overcoming Negative Influences

2 Timothy 2:22 (KJV) "Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart."

1–2. The Influence of Friends; Identifying Negative Peer Pressure

Peer pressure can push students to cheat, bully, skip classes, or break school rules. Students do not have to follow the crowd.

3. Fleeing Youthful Temptations

Wisdom sometimes means walking away from a situation rather than staying close to temptation.

4. Choosing Friends Who Encourage Positive Growth

A good friend encourages, corrects, respects, and celebrates your success without jealousy.

5–6. Pursuing Righteousness and Building Courage to Say "No"

Saying no to wrongdoing is not weakness — it is courage.

Evaluation

  1. What is peer pressure?
  2. Mention four qualities of a good friend.
  3. Give two examples of how to say no to peer pressure.

Conclusion: Real courage is not following the crowd; it is having the strength to stand for what is right.

Final Weekly Conclusion The ultimate goal is not merely to become better than another student. It is to become a better version of yourself, develop your God-given abilities, make wise decisions, and live a life that honours God.

Week 8: Priorities

Day 1 — Monday

Putting God First

Mark 12:28 (KJV) "And one of the scribes came, and having heard them reasoning together, and perceiving that he had answered them well, asked him, Which is the first commandment of all?"

1. Understanding the Meaning of Priorities

A priority is something considered more important than other things and deserving attention first. The important question is: Are my priorities in the right order?

2. Making God the First Priority

Putting God first means seeking Him through prayer, reading and obeying His Word, and making decisions according to biblical principles.

3. Balancing Spiritual Life With School Responsibilities

Faith should encourage students to become more responsible, not neglect responsibilities.

4. Avoiding the Wrong Priorities

Entertainment, social media, popularity, and fashion should not control a student's life.

Evaluation

  1. What is a priority?
  2. Mention three ways a student can put God first.
  3. What is one priority you need to change?

Conclusion: Right priorities begin with putting God first.

Day 2 — Tuesday

Prioritising Your Education and Future

Matthew 6:19 (KJV) "Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal."

1–2. The Value of Education; Making Academic Work a Priority

Education equips students with knowledge, skills, and discipline for future opportunities. Being a student means education is a major responsibility.

3. Avoiding Procrastination

Delaying study until an examination is near creates pressure and poor results.

4. Investing in Your Future

Knowledge, wisdom, good character, and a strong relationship with God are valuable, lasting investments.

5–6. Setting Academic Goals; Managing Distractions

The goal is not to eliminate recreation but to ensure it doesn't replace responsibility.

Evaluation

  1. Why is education important?
  2. What is procrastination and how does it affect performance?
  3. Mention one academic goal you intend to achieve.

Conclusion: A student who prioritises learning today is preparing for greater opportunities tomorrow.

Day 3 — Wednesday

Controlling Worry and Unnecessary Desires

Luke 12:29 (KJV) "And seek not ye what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink, neither be ye of doubtful mind."

1–2. Understanding Worry; Learning to Trust God

Trusting God does not mean refusing to work hard — it means doing your responsibility while refusing to let fear control you.

3. Controlling Material Desires

A need is something essential; a want is desirable but not essential. Understanding this builds contentment.

4. Avoiding Comparison With Others

What we see online does not always represent the complete reality of another person's life.

5. Developing Contentment

Being grateful for what you have without becoming lazy or refusing to improve.

Evaluation

  1. What is the difference between a need and a want?
  2. Why should students avoid comparing themselves with others?
  3. Mention three things you are grateful for.

Conclusion: When your priorities are right, you learn to value what truly matters.

Day 4 — Thursday

Making Prayer a Priority

Psalm 5:3 (KJV) "My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up."

1–2. The Importance of Prayer; Starting the Day With God

Prayer should not only happen during an examination or problem — it should become part of daily life.

3. Developing a Consistent Prayer Life

Morning: thank God and commit the day. During the day: pray silently over decisions. Evening: reflect and ask for guidance.

4. Combining Prayer With Action

Prayer does not replace responsibility — pray for wisdom, then study; pray for success, then work diligently.

5. Looking to God for Direction

Prayer helps students seek God's guidance rather than deciding purely from peer pressure or emotion.

Evaluation

  1. What is prayer, and why should students make it a priority?
  2. Create a simple daily prayer routine.

Conclusion: When prayer becomes a priority, students learn to begin and continue their activities with God.

Day 5 — Friday

Prioritising Fellowship and Positive Relationships

Hebrews 10:25 (KJV) "Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching."

1–2. The Importance of Fellowship; Attending Church and Christian Fellowship

Christian students do not have to grow spiritually alone.

3. Learning From Positive Relationships

Good relationships encourage students to study, pray, and make wise choices.

4. Encouraging Others

Students should encourage struggling classmates and speak words that build people up.

5–6. Avoiding Isolation; Balancing Fellowship With Other Responsibilities

Students should seek help from trustworthy adults when necessary, while balancing fellowship with schoolwork.

Evaluation

  1. What is fellowship, and why is it important?
  2. Mention three ways you can encourage someone this week.
Overall Conclusion "What is most important, and am I giving it the attention it deserves?" When students establish godly priorities, manage time, and build positive relationships, they become better prepared for present responsibilities and future success.

Week 9: Understanding Our Parents

Day 1 — Monday

Who Are Our Parents and What Is Their Role?

Proverbs 22:6 (KJV) "Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it."

1. Understanding Who Our Parents Are

Parents care for, protect, guide, teach, and support their children, helping them develop values, character, and responsible behaviour.

2. The Roles of Parents

Providers, protectors, teachers, guides, disciplinarians, and encouragers.

3. Why Parents Set Rules and Boundaries

Rules provide structure and safety around school attendance, bedtime, phone usage, friends, and chores.

4–5. Parents' Sacrifices and Their Own Challenges

Parents can become tired, worried, or stressed — understanding this helps students become more patient and compassionate.

6. Appreciating Parental Sacrifices

Saying thank you, helping with chores, studying seriously, and speaking respectfully.

Evaluation

  1. Mention five responsibilities of parents.
  2. Mention three ways you can show appreciation to your parents.

Conclusion: Understanding the role of parents helps children appreciate the love, responsibility, and sacrifice involved in raising them.

Day 2 — Tuesday

Understanding Parental Guidance and Values

Deuteronomy 6:6 (KJV) "And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart."

1. Parents as Teachers of Values

Honesty, respect, responsibility, kindness, hard work, faith, self-control, good manners.

Understanding Correction and Discipline

Proverbs 13:24 (KJV) "He that spareth his rod hateth his son: but he that loveth him chasteneth him betimes."

1. The Purpose of Correction

Correction helps a person recognise wrong behaviour and make better choices.

2. Correction vs. Abuse

Healthy discipline teaches and corrects. Violence causing serious harm is not acceptable — a student experiencing serious abuse should seek help from a trusted adult, teacher, or appropriate service.

3–4. Consequences and Learning From Correction

When corrected, ask: What did I do wrong? What should I do differently?

5–6. Avoiding Rebellion; Responding Properly

A mature response: "I understand. I am sorry for what I did. I will try not to repeat it."

Week 10: Examination Culture — Part II

Day 1 — Monday

Developing the Right Examination Mindset

An examination mindset is the way a student thinks, feels, and behaves toward examinations. Confidence means believing that with proper preparation and God's help, one can perform well — not assuming success will come without studying. Students should set specific, measurable, realistic academic goals and avoid negative thoughts, remembering that one poor result does not define them.

Evaluation

  1. What is an examination mindset?
  2. State two ways students can overcome examination anxiety.

Conclusion: Success begins with the right attitude before the examination.

Day 2 — Tuesday

Effective Study and Revision Techniques

Effective study means learning to understand, remember, and apply — not just spending hours with books. Students should read actively, take notes, use past questions to identify weak areas (not just memorise them), study in groups, ask teachers for help, and revise at intervals rather than cramming.

Evaluation

  1. Mention three effective revision techniques.
  2. Why are past questions useful?

Conclusion: Good preparation gives students greater confidence when they enter the examination hall.

Day 3 — Wednesday

Time Management During Examinations

Students should carefully read instructions, plan time according to the number of questions, and reserve time to review answers. Starting with questions they understand builds confidence; if stuck on a difficult question, they should move on and return later where the rules allow.

Evaluation

  1. Why should students read examination instructions carefully?
  2. What should a student do when faced with a difficult question?

Conclusion: A calm and organized student is more likely to use examination time effectively.

Day 4 — Thursday

Examination Ethics and Avoiding Malpractice

Examination malpractice includes copying, unauthorised devices, impersonation, and receiving outside assistance. Causes include fear of failure and peer pressure; consequences include cancellation of results, suspension, or expulsion. The best alternative to cheating is preparation — attending classes, revising, and seeking help when needed.

Evaluation

  1. Mention four forms of examination malpractice.
  2. How can students resist peer pressure to cheat?

Conclusion: True success is achieved when good results are obtained through genuine effort.

Day 5 — Friday

Preparing for Success Before, During and After Examinations

Students need adequate sleep, balanced meals, and personal discipline. They should bring only permitted materials, stay calm and focused on their own paper, and present answers clearly with correct numbering and workings shown where needed. After results, they should examine performance honestly and turn mistakes into lessons for the future.

Evaluation

  1. Why is adequate sleep important before an examination?
  2. Why should students learn from their examination results?
Conclusion Success in examinations comes from preparation, discipline, effective study, proper time management, honesty, confidence, and perseverance. With consistent effort and the right attitude, students can achieve their academic goals.
